Territory

Trincheira Bacajá Indigenous Territory

An Indigenous Territory in the Xingu basin, in Pará, Brazil, crossed by the Bacajá River. Most villages lie along its banks.

1.65Mhectares (1,650,939 ha) of officially recognized Indigenous Territory, for the exclusive use of the Xikrin people
98%or more of the territory preserved
53villages in the Territory, represented by six Xikrin associations
16Berê villages, in Anapu and Senador José Porfírio

Sources: Annex 1 of the Restaura Amazônia project and the Association’s Panorama (2026).

Pressure comes from outside

Standing forest, threatened edges

The interior of the Indigenous Territory is preserved. The pressure comes from its surroundings: deforestation, past invasions by loggers, illegal gold mining, land grabbing at the borders and the effects of large infrastructure projects.

The Belo Monte dam changed the river’s flow. The Xikrin say that “the Xingu River holds the Bacajá River”: when the Xingu rises, the Bacajá is full; when it falls, the Bacajá drops with it. In the reduced-flow stretch of the Xingu, floods fell from 80 to 9 days a year, and extreme droughts rose from 45 to 104 days (Revista Pesquisa FAPESP).

The forest provides Brazil nuts, babassu, cacao, açaí, cumaru, andiroba, copaíba and many other resources that sustain the families.
